# Real Estate NJ's 2026 Market Forecast

**Published:** January 15, 2026 — **Author:** Joshua Burd (Real Estate NJ)

New Jersey commercial real estate experts anticipate stronger market activity in 2026 following volatility in 2025; the consensus is that "capital and confidence had returned to the market in a way not seen in several years."

## By Sector

- **Multifamily — the market leader.** CBRE forecasts transaction volumes exceeding 2025, with cap-rate stabilization and garden-style value-add assets preferred; returning institutional capital and bank financing as drivers; new-construction rents expected to level off "except in high-demand submarkets." Kislak emphasizes sustained housing demand, limited new supply in many submarkets, and homeownership affordability challenges supporting multifamily resilience.
- **Office.** Bullish sentiment on trophy Class A — redefined as "walkable locations with integrated lifestyle amenities rather than aesthetic appeal alone." Office otherwise "a bit of a question mark" but with "positive traction."
- **Industrial.** Increased transaction volume on lower rates and tariff stability; rents "flat" with concessions early; vacancy decreasing.
- **Retail.** "More resilient than many expected"; neighborhood centers and Main Street locations anchored by food, service, fitness, and dining performing well. Leasing "on the upswing."
- **Data centers.** Significant upswing in design and feasibility studies; power distribution "a major bottleneck."

## Affordable Housing & Development — the "A4" wave

The landmark "A4" legislation reformed New Jersey's affordable-housing framework. Municipal fair-share obligations were established in 2025, with zoning requirements mandated by March 15, 2026. One contributor projects "massive construction will occur in New Jersey for the next several years." A4 proceedings have generated obligations to rezone for thousands of new homes — mostly multifamily and mixed-use — increasing both market-rate and affordable supply, though single-family development still faces land scarcity.

## Capital & Rates

Institutional capital and conventional lenders have returned, with "dry powder" ready for deployment; the 10-year Treasury near 4% supports debt and equity liquidity; reinstated 100% bonus depreciation enhances buyer returns. Construction-cost inflation from 2025 is stabilizing; insurance premiums driven by storm frequency remain a pressure.
