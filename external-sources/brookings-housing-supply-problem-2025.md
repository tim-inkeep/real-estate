# Brookings - America's Housing Supply Problem

Source note for downstream research. Full source URL is preserved in frontmatter.

## Captured source facts

- Final version date shown by source: December 2025; conference draft discussed March 2025.
- Brookings summarizes Edward Glaeser and Joseph Gyourko's housing-supply study covering Census data from 1950 to 2023.
- The study argues Sun Belt markets such as Miami and Phoenix are increasingly resembling high-priced coastal markets because construction rates slowed.
- Brookings reports that the U.S. housing stock growth rate slowed from 4% in the 1950s to 0.6% in the 2010s.
- Brookings reports Phoenix housing-stock growth fell from 9.1% annually in the 1970s to 1.0% in the 2010s, while Los Angeles was 0.5% in the 2010s.
- The authors conclude that decreased supply is responsible for increased prices, with rates and production costs only partial explanations.

## Use in research

Use this only as historical context for 2026: the old Sun Belt affordability valve is less reliable when fast-growth metros no longer add enough housing cheaply and quickly.
