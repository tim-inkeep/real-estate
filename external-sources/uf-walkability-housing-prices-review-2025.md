# The Value of Living Within Walking Distance

**Author:** Arijit Ray (UF Warrington College of Business) — **Date:** March 19, 2025

## What Walkability Measures

Walkability encompasses pedestrian infrastructure, amenities/services, density and mixed-use development, and safety. Walk Score (developed 2007) rates areas 0–100. Top-rated walkable cities: San Francisco (89), New York (88), Boston (86).

## Research Review: Walkability and Housing Prices

**Positive correlations:**

- A 2020 Redfin study of nearly 1 million homes across 18 major metros found "the average sales prices of homes in highly walkable neighborhoods was 23.5% more, roughly $77,668, than comparable homes in car-dependent areas."
- A CEOs for Cities study of 90,000 home sales across 15 markets found higher Walk Scores were generally associated with higher home values, "though this relationship was not universal."

**Limited or negative impact (studies that control for local characteristics):**

- **Boyle et al. (2014)** examined single-family homes and condominiums in Miami and determined that "walkability does not have any effect on housing prices" once local characteristics are controlled for — the statistical significance of the relationship vanishes.
- **Chernobai and Ma (2022)** studied Orange County, California single-family homes: walkability had a statistically significant impact on houses with no garage or one garage space, but the effect diminished or reversed for homes with two or more garage spaces (potentially due to traffic noise).
- **Dey et al. (2023)** examined the Atlanta metro area and found "walkability alone is negatively associated with housing prices," though neighborhood demographic composition influenced whether walkability benefits certain buyer groups.

**Commercial properties:**

- Pivo and Fisher (2011) analyzed over 4,000 properties: a positive correlation between walkability and office, retail, and apartment values, but no correlation with industrial properties.

**Mortgage default risk:**

- Pivo (2014) examined 37,000 multifamily mortgages: properties with a Walk Score under 80 had a 60% higher default rate than those over 80; properties scoring 8 or less had a 121% higher default rate.

## Key Takeaway

"While walkability serves as a proxy for desirable urban amenities and shows correlation with housing values in dense, mixed-use markets, the relationship is complex and context-dependent. Real estate professionals should consider walkability alongside other factors rather than as a standalone investment criterion."
