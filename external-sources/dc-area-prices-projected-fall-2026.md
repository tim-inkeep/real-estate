# D.C. Area Home Prices Projected to Fall in 2026 as Federal Uncertainty Weighs on Market

**By Scott McCaffrey (ARLnow)** — **Published:** December 4, 2025

The D.C. metro area stands alone in the Mid-Atlantic as the only region where home prices are expected to fall next year.

A forecast from multiple-listing service Bright MLS (issued Dec. 3, 2025) predicts the median sales price in the D.C. region in 2026 will drop 1% to $616,700, following a projected 3% increase in 2025.

The culprit: federal government uncertainty. "Ongoing uncertainty around the federal government suggests weaker demand in the Washington D.C. metro," the forecast notes. "More affordable markets [across the Mid-Atlantic] are expected to see stronger sales and faster home price growth in 2026."

By contrast, every other major Mid-Atlantic market is projected to see price growth of 1.5%–3.1%: Philadelphia's median expected to climb 2.8% to $400,360; Baltimore up 2.5% to $412,100; north-central Virginia up 1.5% to $505,980.

Despite the price dip, the D.C. area is not expected to see fewer transactions — total sales are projected to rise 9.6% to 55,650 homes, as mortgage rates ease and inventory loosens (Bright MLS projects rates falling to ~6.15% by end of 2026).

Across the Mid-Atlantic, the median sales price is anticipated at $436,270 in 2026 (+2.6%). Nationally, Bright MLS cites a 2026 median of $417,600, up 0.9% from 2025. "Still-low inventory will make the region's housing market more competitive, and sellers will still have an advantage in many local markets."
