# The State of Walkable Urbanism in the Top U.S. Metro Areas: 2023 "Foot Traffic Ahead" Report Summary

**Published:** March 28, 2023

## Key Findings

The 2023 "Foot Traffic Ahead" report — a collaboration between Yardi Matrix, Smart Growth America, and Places Platform LLC — examined walkability across the 35 largest U.S. metropolitan regions.

**Walkability distribution:** Walkable urbanism comprised only 1% to 7% of metro land masses, averaging 1.2% across the 35 largest metros. Those compact areas generated 19.1% of U.S. real GDP while housing 6.8% of the residential population.

**Price premiums:** Demand significantly exceeded supply. For-sale housing commanded a 34% price premium in walkable areas; commercial properties exceeded 40%. The report noted price gaps **narrowed in 26 of 35 markets since 2018**, with for-sale housing gaps closing in only six post-industrial cities by 2021.

**Real estate concentration by product type:** Office 42% in walkable areas; multifamily rental 30%; retail 19%; for-sale housing 12%. New York led — 73% of office space and 70% of multifamily inventory in walkable zones.

## Metro Rankings

- **Tier 1 (highest walkable urbanism):** New York, Boston, Washington D.C., Seattle, Portland, San Francisco, Chicago, Los Angeles
- **Tier 2 (upper-middle):** Pittsburgh, Philadelphia; Denver, Minneapolis-St. Paul
- **Tier 3 (lower-middle):** Columbus (highest in tier), Baltimore, Kansas City, St. Louis, Cincinnati, Detroit — constrained by limited rail service
- **Tier 4 (lowest):** Primarily Sunbelt metros, including Las Vegas (ranked last, walkability concentrated mainly on The Strip)

## Methodology & Data

The Foot Traffic Ahead Index measured combined office, retail, multifamily, and for-sale housing shares in Type I or Type II walkable urbanism, weighting commercial space more heavily than residential. The report also introduced a Social Equity Index (SEI) assessing affordability, transit quality, and proximity to walkability; Cleveland, Kansas City, Detroit, Pittsburgh, and Baltimore ranked highest for equitable access.

Data sources: Yardi Matrix databases, Places Platform LLC, Rocktop Partners LLC, AEI Housing Center, REIS Moody's, U.S. Census Bureau, EPA Smart Location Database, U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis. Limitation: the report excluded owner-user commercial space (an estimated 30–40% of total commercial real estate).

## Economic Implications

The report concludes walkable urbanism is positioned to drive 21st-century economic growth comparable to suburban development's 20th-century impact, and finds higher education levels and one-third higher GDP per capita in high-ranking metros.
