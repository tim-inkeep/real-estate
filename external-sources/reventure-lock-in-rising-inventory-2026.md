# The End of 3% Mortgages: Why the Mortgage Lock-In Effect Is Fading in 2026

Source note for downstream research. Full source URL is preserved in frontmatter.

## Captured source facts

- Publication date shown by source: December 6, 2025.
- Reventure argues that inventory rose in 2024-2025 and that the mortgage-rate lock-in effect was weakening going into 2026.
- The source cites Realtor.com active listings around 1.1 million, within 9% of pre-pandemic inventory nationally, with some Sun Belt and West Coast markets already past that normalization point.
- Reventure identifies weakening rents as a key valuation anchor because falling rents can reduce investor demand and make buying look worse relative to renting.
- Reventure flags markets with declining or stagnant rents, high investor concentration, large new multifamily supply, rapid pandemic price appreciation, and elevated price-to-income or price-to-rent ratios as most exposed in 2026.

## Use in research

Use this as Reventure's supply/rent mechanism: rising resale inventory plus weak rental fundamentals creates downside risk, especially in oversupplied pandemic-boom metros.
