# Philadelphia Real Estate Market Overview & Forecast (2026)

**Published:** April 10, 2026 — **Updated:** May 2026 — **Author:** Savvas Agathangelou

The 2026 Philadelphia market is "maintaining pace with high-cost Northeast markets while preserving a meaningfully lower price floor." Knight Frank's 2026 US Cities Prime Index ranks Philadelphia among the best-value major metros on the East Coast.

## Key Metrics

- Median sale price: $245,000 (+4.3% YoY); median list price $260,000
- Average days on market: 31 (15–20 in inner-ring areas); sale-to-list ratio 99.1%
- Strongest demand: Center City, Fairmount, Old City, Northern Liberties
- Home prices projected to appreciate 4–6% through 2026

Buyers from New York and Boston "continue redirecting capital here for value, drawn by Center City revival, university presence, and regional rail infrastructure." Realtor.com's 2026 Hottest Markets list placed Philadelphia in the top-20 large-metro category for three consecutive months.

## Neighborhoods

- Center City — median condo ~$545,000; median listing $495,000 ($380/sqft)
- Fairmount — median $420,000 (+3.8% YoY)
- Old City — median condo $530,000
- Northern Liberties — median $475,000 (+4.1% YoY)
- University City — median ~$410,000; tightest rental dynamics
- Value tiers: West Philadelphia $210,000; North Philadelphia $195,000

## Rental Market

Average metro rent $1,580/month (+3.7% YoY); Center City one-bedrooms $1,950–$2,400. Vacancy tight at 4.8%. Rents forecast to climb 3.5–5% in 2026.

## Market-Shaping Forces

1. **Institutional employment** — Penn, Drexel, Temple, Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, Jefferson Health provide a stable demand floor.
2. **Center City revival** — SEPTA rail backbone and walkability attract younger renters and family-formation buyers.
3. **Capital reallocation** — steady high-net-worth acquisition of Center City townhomes, especially since the post-2022 work-from-anywhere cycle.

Inventory below $400,000 clears quickly; $1M-plus stock requires longer searches. Philadelphia is described as "a structurally undervalued market beginning honest repricing."
